# Course 2: Ask Questions to Make Data-Driven Decisions

## Craft Effective Questions

| Concept                       | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Leading Question**          | - A question that leads you to answer a certain way                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Close-Ended Question**      | - A question that can be answered with a yes or no                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Ineffective Questions**     | - Leading questions<br>- Close-ended questions<br>- Questions that are too vague and lack context                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Effective Questions**       | - Follow the SMART methodology<br>- **S**pecific<br>- **M**easurable<br>- **A**ction-oriented<br>- **R**elevant<br>- **T**ime-bound                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Specific Questions**        | - Specific questions are simple, significant, and focused on a single topic or a few closely related ideas<br>&emsp;• This helps us collect information that's relevant to what we're investigating<br><br>- If a question is too general, try to narrow it down by focusing on just one element<br><br>- **Example:** instead of "are kids getting enough physical activities these days?", ask "what percentage of kids achieve the recommended 60 minutes of physical activity at least five days a week?"<br><br>- **Questions to ask yourself:**<br>&emsp;• Is the question specific? <br>&emsp;• Does it address the problem? <br>&emsp;• Does it have context? <br>&emsp;• Will it uncover a lot of the information you need? |
| **Measurable Questions**      | - Can be quantified and assessed <br><br>- **Example:** Instead of "why did a recent video go viral?", ask "how many times was our video shared on social channels the first week it was posted?"<br>&emsp;• In the example, the question is measurable because it lets us count the shares and arrive at a concrete number.<br><br>- **Questions to ask yourself:**<br>&emsp;• Will the question give you answers that you can measure?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Action-Oriented Questions** | - Questions that encourage change <br>- These questions allow you to see the current state and figure out how to transform it to the ideal future state<br><br>- **Example:** Instead of "how can we get customers to recycle our product packaging?" You can ask, "What design features will make our packaging easier to recycle?"<br>&emsp;• This brings you answers you can act on<br><br>- **Questions to ask yourself:**<br>&emsp;• Will the answers provide information that helps you devise some type of plan?                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Relevant Questions**        | - Questions that matter, are important, and have significance to the problem you're trying to solve<br><br>- **Example:** Let's say you're working on a problem related to a threatened species of frog. If you ask "why does it matter that Pine Barrens tree frogs started disappearing?", the answer won't help us find a way to prevent the frogs from going extinct.<br>&emsp;• A more relevant question would be, "Durham, ​North Carolina between 1983 and 2004 that could cause ​Pine Barrens tree frogs to disappear from the Sandhills Regions?"<br><br>- **Questions to ask yourself:**<br>&emsp;• Is the question about the particular problem you are trying to solve?                                                  |
| **Time-Bound Questions**      | - Specify the time to be studied <br>- This limits the range of possibilities and enables a data analyst to focus<br><br>- **Questions to ask yourself:**<br>&emsp;• Are the answers relevant to the specific time being studied?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Fairness**                  | - Ensuring that your questions don't create or reinforce bias<br>&emsp;• Craft questions that make sense to everyone<br>&emsp;• Don't lead folks into a certain answer<br>&emsp;• Ensure your questions don't make unfair assumptions                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Unfair Question**           | - A question that makes assumptions or is difficult to answer honestly                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

## Summary

Effective questions follow the SMART methodology. These are Specific, Measurable, Action-oriented, Relevant, Time-bound questions. Specific questions must clearly define the single topic/subject and the single measurement/metric being analyzed. Specificity requires a narrow scope, centering on one main topic rather than a broad organizational goal. Additionally, a specific question is easy to understand and avoids vague language or multiple interpretations.

Relevant questions matter, are important, and have significance to the problem. The question, “How can we create a more innovative culture by empowering our employees to take risks?” is action-oriented because it encourages change.

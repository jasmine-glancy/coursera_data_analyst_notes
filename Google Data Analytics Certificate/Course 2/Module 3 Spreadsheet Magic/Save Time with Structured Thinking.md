## Summary

A problem domain is the term for the specific area of analysis that encompasses every activity affecting or affected by a problem. Structured thinking is the process of recognizing the current problem or situation, organizing available information, revealing gaps and opportunities, and identifying the options. Implementing a solution comes later. 

A scope of work is an agreed-upon outline of the work you’re going to perform on a project. Common focus areas in a Scope of Work include: noting when a client can expect reports, include information about analysis of quantitative and qualitative datasets, and establish an agreed-upon outline of the work to be performed. To ensure accurate and fair data, ensure an accurate representation of the population, collect data in an impartial manager, and consider different viewpoints before drawing conclusions.

SMART questions about your datasets help you know where to start when formulating your scope of work. The more questions you ask about your data, the more you will understand its limitations and how it can assist you. When I tried to craft an SOW before determining SMART questions, it was much more difficult trying to guess what I my stakeholders would want to know, especially without looking at the data.

The Scope of Work (SOW) is mostly an organizational tool to help analysts make effective and impactful decisions. It’s about being technical by taking the same steps each time to make sure the analyst acts with intention. Being able to describe your process and the methods highlights your ability to be thorough, follow through on projects, and apply effective frameworks to your work.

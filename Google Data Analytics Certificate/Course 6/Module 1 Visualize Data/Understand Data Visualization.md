| **Marks**                                      | - Basic visual objects such as points, lines, and shapes.  <br>- Every mark can be broken down into four qualities:<br>&emsp; 1. **Position:** Where is a specific mark relative to a scale or other marks?<br>&emsp; 2. **Size:** How big, small, long, or tall is a mark? <br>&emsp;&emsp;• The human eye can inadvertently interpret comparisons that aren’t intended to convey meaning<br>&emsp;&emsp;• For example, sometimes objects that appear to be the same size when they are not<br>    <br>&emsp;• 4. **Shape:** Does the shape of a specific object communicate something about it?<br>&emsp;• 5. **Color:** What color is a mark?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Channels**                                   | - Visual aspects or variables that represent characteristics of data in a visualization<br>- Specialized marks that have been used to visualize data <br>- Vary in terms of how effective they are at communicating based on three elements:<br>&emsp; 1. **Accuracy:** Are the channels helpful in accurately estimating the values being represented?<br>&emsp;&emsp;• Example: <br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• Color is very accurate when communicating categorical differences, such as apples and oranges<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• But it is much less effective when distinguishing quantitative data, such as 5 from 5.5<br>    <br>&emsp; 2. **Popout:** How easy is it to distinguish certain values from others?<br>&emsp;&emsp;• Many ways to draw attention to specific parts of the visual:<br>&emsp;&emsp;• Leverage pre-attentive attributes including line length, size, line width, shape, enclosure, hue, and intensity<br><br>&emsp; 3. **Grouping:** How effective is a channel at communicating groups that exist in the data?<br>&emsp;&emsp;• Consider the proximity, similarity, enclosure, connectedness, and continuity of the channel.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

| **Decision Tree**                              | - A decision-making tool that allows you, the data analyst, to make decisions based on key questions that you can ask yourself<br>    <br>1. Does your data have only one numeric variable?<br>&emsp;• If you have data that has one, continuous, numerical variable, then a histogram or density plot are the best methods of plotting your categorical data<br>&emsp;• Depending on your type of data, a bar chart can even be appropriate in this case<br>    <br>2. Are there multiple datasets?<br>&emsp;• A line chart will connect multiple data sets over a single, continuous line, showing how numbers have changed over time<br>&emsp;• A pie chart is good for dividing a whole into multiple categories or parts<br><br>3. Are you measuring changes over time?<br>&emsp;• A line chart is usually adequate for plotting trends over time<br>&emsp;• However, when the changes are larger, a bar chart is the better option <br><br>4.   ## Summary

The x-axis is a horizontal line used to represent categories, time periods, or other variables. The y-axis is a vertical line that usually has a scale of values for variables. Bar graphs use size contrast to compare two or more values, while pie charts use segments of a circle to represent the proportions of each data category compared to the whole. Correlation charts show relationships among data. Causation occurs when an action directly leads to an outcome. Dynamic visualizations are data visualizations that are interactive or change over time.

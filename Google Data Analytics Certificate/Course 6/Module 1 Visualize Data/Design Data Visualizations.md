| **Nine Basic Principles of Design**               | - **Things to consider while you are creating your data visualization:**<br>&emsp;&emsp;• ​1. Balance<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• ​When the key visual elements, like color and shape, are distributed evenly<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• ​ i.e. lines used to create the graphics are similar in length on both sides or that the space between objects is equal<br>    <br>&emsp;&emsp;• ​2. Proportion<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• ​Using various colors and sizes helps demonstrate that you are calling attention to a specific visual over others<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• ​If you make one chart in a dashboard larger than the others, then you are calling attention to it<br>&emsp;&emsp;&emsp;• ​It is important to make sure that each chart accurately reflects and visualizes the relationship among the values in it<br>    <br>- **Useful checks once your data visualization is finished:**<br>&emsp;&emsp;• ​7. ## Summary

Refined execution means paying deep attention to detail. This is done by using visual elements such as lines, shapes, colors, value, space, and movement. Design thinking is a process used to solve complex problems in a user-centric way. User-centricity means considering the user and their needs first. A line can add visual form to data and help build the structure of a visualization. Intensity describes how bright or dull a color is within a data visualization. Design thinking is a process used to solve complex problems in a user-centric way. It enables data analysts to identify alternative strategies for visualizations. Considering appropriate colors for a data visualization is part of the empathize stage, which involves thinking about the emotions and needs of your audience.

# Module 1: Visualize Data

## Visualization Considerations

| Concept                                         | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Headline**                                    | - A line of words printed in large letters at the top of a visualization to communicate what data is being presented<br>- It is the attention grabber that makes your audience want to read more<br>- Without a headline, it can be hard to figure out what data is being presented<br>- ***Guidelines***<br>&emsp;• **Content:** Briefly describe the data<br>&emsp;• **Length:** Usually the width of the data frame<br>&emsp;• **Position:** Above the data<br><br>- ***Style Checks***<br>&emsp;• Use brief language <br>&emsp;• Don’t use all caps<br>&emsp;• Don’t use italic<br>&emsp;• Don’t use acronyms<br>&emsp;• Don't use abbreviations<br>&emsp;• Don’t use humor or sarcasm                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Subtitle**                                    | - Supports the headline by adding more context and description<br>- Adding a subtitle will help the audience better understand the details associated with your chart<br>- Typically, the text for subtitles has a smaller font size than the headline    <br>- ***Guidelines***<br>&emsp;• **Content:** Clarify context for the data<br>&emsp;• **Length:** Same as or shorter than headline <br>&emsp;• **Position:** Directly below the headline<br>    <br>- ***Style Checks***<br>&emsp;• Use smaller font size than headline<br>&emsp;• Don’t use undefined words <br>&emsp;• Don’t use all caps, bold, or italic<br>&emsp;• Don’t use acronyms <br>&emsp;• Don't use abbreviations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Label**                                       | - Identifies data in relation to other data <br>- ***Guidelines***<br>&emsp;• **Content:** Replace the need for legends<br>&emsp;• **Length:** Usually fewer than 30 characters<br>&emsp;• **Position:** Next to data or below or beside axes<br><br>- ***Style Checks***<br>&emsp;• Use a few words only<br>&emsp;• Use thoughtful color-coding<br>&emsp;• Use callouts to point to the data<br>&emsp;• Don’t use all caps, bold, or italic                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Annotation**                                  | - Briefly explains data or helps focus the audience on a particular aspect of the data in a visualization <br>- ***Guidelines***<br>&emsp;• **Content:** Draw attention to certain data <br>&emsp;• **Length:** Varies, limited by open space<br>&emsp;• **Position:** Immediately next to data annotated<br><br>- ***Style Checks***<br>&emsp;• Don’t use all caps, bold, or italic<br>&emsp;• Don't use rotated text<br>&emsp;• Don’t distract viewers from the data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Ways to make Data Visualizations Accessible** | - **Labeling** data directly instead of relying on legends, which require color interpretation<br>- **Text alternatives** so it can be changed into other forms people need such as large print, braille, or speech<br>- **Text-based format** to turn data from charts and diagrams to a text-based format through an export to Sheets or Excel<br>- **Distinguishing** the content from the foreground and background<br>- **Red-green color blindness** is the most common and occurs when red and green look like the same color<br>&emsp;• You can avoid placing green on red or red on green in your visualizations<br>    <br>- **Blue-yellow color blindness** is less common and occurs when it is difficult to tell the difference between blue and green, or yellow and red<br>&emsp;• You can also avoid using these colors on top of or next to each other<br><br>&emsp;• For more information, refer to these resources:<br>&emsp;&emsp;• [Types of Color Blindness](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness)<br>&emsp;&emsp;• [Web Accessibility Guidelines, Contrast & Color](http://web-accessibility.carnegiemuseums.org/design/color/)<br>    <br><br>- **Simplify** visualizations<br>&emsp;• Complicated visualizations can make it difficult to focus |
| **Alternative Text**                            | - Provides a textual alternative to non-text content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Design a Chart in 60 Minutes**                | - Prep (5 min)<br>&emsp;• Create the mental and physical space necessary for an environment of comprehensive thinking<br>&emsp;• This means allowing yourself room to brainstorm *how* you want your data to appear while considering the amount and type of data that you have<br>    <br>- Talk and listen (15 min)<br>&emsp;• Identify the object of your work by getting to the “ask behind the ask” and establishing expectations<br>&emsp;• Ask questions and really concentrate on feedback from stakeholders regarding your projects to help you hone how to lay out your data. <br>    <br>- Sketch and design (20 min)<br>&emsp;• Draft your approach to the problem<br>&emsp;• Define the timing and output of your work to get a clear and concise idea of what you are crafting.<br><br>- Prototype and improve (20 min)<br>&emsp;• Generate a visual solution and gauge its effectiveness at accurately communicating your data<br>&emsp;• Take your time and repeat the process until a final visual is produced<br>&emsp;• It is alright if you go through several visuals until you find the perfect fit.<br><br>- ***Always remember to take the time to prep, identify your objective, take in feedback, design, and create***                                                                              |

## Summary

The three basic visualization considerations are headlines, subtitles, and labels. Directly labeling a data visualization helps viewers identify data quickly. Legends are often less effective because they are positioned away from the data. Alternative text provides a textual alternative to non-text context. Alternative text ensures that users who need to access your data visualizations in different ways, like with a screen-reader, will still absorb the information. Simplifying your data visualizations can help your audience understand and focus on the important data. To do this, avoid overly complicated visuals or unnecessary information.

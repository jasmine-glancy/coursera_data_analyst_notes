| **Presentation Evaluation Criteria**    | - **Include a title, subtitle, and date:**<br>&emsp;• Makes sure that your audience knows exactly what you are presenting and when the information was from<br>&emsp;• That way they know it’s relevant and current to them!<br>    <br>- **Use a logical sequence of slides:** <br>&emsp;• Organizing your slides in an order that makes sense guides your audience through your narrative, building understanding step by step<br><br>- **Provide an agenda with a timeline:** <br>&emsp;• Offers a roadmap of your presentation<br>&emsp;• Allows your audience to follow along and anticipate key topics<br>    <br>- **Limit the amount of text on slides:** <br>&emsp;• Keeping text brief ensures clarity and retains the audience’s attention<br>&emsp;• aim for your audience to scan it within 5 seconds.<br><br>- **Start with the business task:** <br>&emsp;• By immediately relating the content to the business task at hand, you contextualize your information, making it relevant and actionable<br>    <br>- **Establish the initial hypothesis:** <br>&emsp;• Presenting an initial hypothesis gives your audience a starting point for what to expect and frames the subsequent analysis<br>    <br>- **Show what business metrics you used:**<br>&emsp;• Clarifying which metrics you're analyzing validates your arguments and helps the audience gauge your presentation's relevance to business outcomes<br>    <br>- **Use visualizations:** <br>&emsp;• Visual aids can illustrate complex data more effectively than text alone, making your message more accessible.<br>    <br>- **Introduce the graphic by name:** <br>&emsp;• A brief introduction to each graphic aids in understanding and retaining information<br>    <br>- **Provide a title for each graph:** <br>&emsp;• Titles act as signposts, helping the audience quickly grasp the meaning of each visual<br>    <br>- **Go from the general to the specific:** <br>&emsp;• Starting with a broad overview before diving into details ensures that all audience members are on the same page<br><br>- **Use speaker notes to help you remember talking points:** <br>&emsp;• Notes act as your cue cards, enabling a smoother delivery and ensuring no critical point is missed.<br>    <br>- **Include key takeaways:** <br>&emsp;• Summarizing the main points at the end of your presentation reinforces the message and ensures the audience leaves with the intended takeaways |

## Summary

The purpose of a framework is to create logical connections that tie back to the business task. It also gives your audience context about your data and helps you focus on the most important information. Before adding data visualizations to a presentation, you should ask yourself if they support the point you are trying to make.

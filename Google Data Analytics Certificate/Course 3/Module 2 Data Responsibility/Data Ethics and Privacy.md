## Data Ethics and Privacy

| Concept                                                             | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ethics**                                                          | - Refers to well-founded standards of right and wrong that prescribe what humans ought to do, usually in terms of rights, obligations, benefits to society, fairness, or specific virtues                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Data Ethics**                                                     | - Well-founded standards of right and wrong that dictate how data is collected, shared, and used<br>- Six aspects:<br>&emsp;1. Ownership<br>&emsp;2. Transaction transparency<br>&emsp;3. Consent<br>&emsp;4. Currency<br>&emsp;5. Privacy<br>&emsp;6. Openness                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **General Data Protection Regulation of the European Union (GDPR)** | - Creates data protection legislation to help protect people and their data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Ownership**                                                       | - Who owns this data? <br>- Individuals own the raw data they provide and they have primary control over its usage, how it’s processed, and how it's shared                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Transaction Transparency**                                        | - All data-processing activities and algorithms should be completely explainable and understood by the individual who provides their data<br>- This is in response to concerns over data bias<br>- ***Biased outcomes can lead to negative consequences**<br>- Transparency lets people judge whether the outcome is fair and unbiased and allows them to raise potential concerns                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Consent**                                                         | - An individual’s right to know explicit details about how and why their data will be used before agreeing to provide it<br>- They should know answers to questions like why is the data being collected?<br>- How will it be used?<br>- How long will it be stored? <br>- Can be a link to terms and conditions with links to more details, but not everyone reads through these details<br>- Consent is important because it prevents all populations from being unfairly targeted, which is a very big deal for marginalized groups |
| **Currency**                                                        | - Individuals should be aware of financial transactions resulting from the use of their personal data and the scale of these transactions<br>- If your data is helping to fund a company’s efforts, you should know what those efforts are and be given the opportunity to opt out                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Privacy**                                                         | - Preserving a data subject’s information and activity any time a data transaction occurs<br>- **Also called information privacy or data protection**<br>- This means:<br>&emsp;•  Protection from unauthorized access to our private data<br>&emsp;• Freedom from inappropriate use of our data<br>&emsp;• The right to inspect, update, ​or correct our data<br>&emsp;• Ability to give consent to use our data, ​<br>&emsp;• Legal right to access our data.                                                                        |
| **Openness**                                                        | - Free access, usage, and sharing of data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Personally Identifiable Information (PII)**                       | - Information that can be used by itself or with other data to track down a person's identity                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Data Anonymization**                                              | - The process of protecting people's private or sensitive data by eliminating that kind of information<br>- Typically, data anonymization involves blanking, hashing, or masking personal information<br>- Done by using fixed-length codes to represent data columns, or hiding data with altered values<br>- Healthcare and financial data are two of the most sensitive types of data                                                                                                                                               |
| **Data that should be Anonymized**                                  | - Data that is usually anonymized:<br>&emsp;• Telephone numbers <br>&emsp;• Names <br>&emsp;• License plates and license numbers <br> &emsp;• Social security numbers <br>&emsp;• IP addresses <br>&emsp;• Medical records <br>&emsp;• Email addresses <br>&emsp;• Photographs <br>&emsp;• Account numbers                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |

## Summary

Data ethics are well-founded standards of right and wrong that dictate how data is collected, shared, and used. At the end of the day, *data are people*. As data folks, we have to prioritize privacy and ethics to protect our people. Consent to gather the data matters. Consent is the aspect of data ethics that presumes an individual’s right to know how and why their personal data will be used, including for how long, before agreeing to provide it. Transaction transparency states that all data-processing activities and algorithms should be completely explainable and understood by the individual who provides their data. Data anonymization is the process of protecting people's private or sensitive data by eliminating identifying information. 

Data privacy, among other things, gives individuals the right to inspect, update, or correct their own data. Good data should be current, comprehensive, and current. To anonymize data, you can hash, blank, or mask data. Interpretation bias is the tendency to interpret ambiguous situations in a positive or negative way. The data ethics principle of transaction transparency states that an individual has the right to understand all of the data-processing activities and algorithms used on their data. Healthcare and financial data are highly sensitive and often undergo data anonymization techniques. To protect patient privacy and financial security by removing personally identifiable information (PII).

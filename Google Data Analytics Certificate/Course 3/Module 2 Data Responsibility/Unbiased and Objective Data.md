# Module 2: Data Responsibility

## Unbiased and Objective Data

| Concept                 | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bias**                | - A preference in favor of or against a person, group of people, or thing <br>&emsp;• Can be conscious or unconscious                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Data Bias**           | - A type of error that systematically skews results in a certain direction <br>&emsp;• Questions may be slanted or potentially a sample group wasn't truly representative of the population being studied<br>&emsp;• Can happen if a sample group lacks inclusivity                                                  |
| **Sampling Bias**       | - A sample isn't representative of the population as a whole <br>&emsp;• You can avoid this by making sure the sample is chosen at random, so all parts of the population have an equal chance of being included<br>&emsp;• If you don't use random sampling during data collection, you end up favoring one outcome |
| **Unbiased Sampling**   | - When a sample is representative of the population being measured                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Observer Bias**       | - The tendency for different people to observe things differently <br> &emsp;• Also known as experimenter bias or research bias                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **Interpretation Bias** | - The tendency to always interpret ambiguous situations in a positive or negative way                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Confirmation Bias**   | - The tendency to search for or interpret information in a way that confirms pre-existing beliefs                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

## Summary

Bias is a conscious or subconscious preference in favor of or against a person, group of people, or thing. Using random sampling during data collection helps ensure unbiased sampling. A survey of students that does not include homeschooled students, an election poll that only interviews people with college degrees, and a clinical study that includes three times more men than women are examples of sampling bias. This is because they are not representative of the population.

The tendency to search for or interpret information in a way that validates pre-existing beliefs is confirmation bias. Observer bias is sometimes referred to as experimenter bias or research bias. Interpretation bias is the tendency to interpret ambiguous situations in a positive or negative way.
